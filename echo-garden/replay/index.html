The arm

From above, the plant sits in the middle with six spots the arm can touch. From the side, you can see the arm dip down. When it touches, the plant's circuit closes and it starts to play.

 

When the notes happen

Each mark is a note. Left alone, the plant sensor ticks like a hidden metronome, about eight times a second. Every played note is nudged off that tick. Turn on the grid and the raw notes line up with it while the played ones don't. There are fewer played notes on purpose, so the echo doesn't get muddy.

The loop

How much of the echo keeps coming back, over the whole session. D briefly turns the echo down, C clears it and P stops everything. The brown bars are the moments the arm touched the plant. Tap anywhere to jump there.

 

Where the notes land

Left, the gaps between notes. Right, where each note lands inside one tick of the plant's hidden metronome. A tall spike means the notes are stuck to the tick. A flat spread means they're free.
