Echo Garden

A gridless instrument for live sets, where the music moves when bodies and plants move.

Picture a potted plant on a wooden table. A small dark box sits beside it, with one cable clipped to a leaf and one pushed into the soil. A desktop robot arm reaches over and rests its tool on a leaf. The box lights up, notes rise from the plant, and slow rings spread from the pot. The arm lifts, the notes stop, and the rings fade.

The 3D view needs WebGL, which this browser does not offer.

Drag to look around. The arm moves to a new leaf every 20 seconds.

A robot arm touches a living plant and closes its circuit. The plant's signal turns into MIDI notes that feed a live Ableton set as one more instrument, so the music gets a voice nobody fully controls. Lift the arm and the plant drops out. Whatever it played can keep circling in a long loop.

How it works

  1. The plant

    A small box called a Pocket Scion reads the plant's electrical signal and turns it into MIDI notes. A threshold sets how dense those notes are, and the same density setting drives other instruments in the set too, such as the sting.

  2. The arm

    When the arm touches a leaf, the circuit closes and the plant's notes come into the set. When it lifts, they stop. On the day of each show the arm has to learn where the leaves are, so it knows where to touch.

  3. The body, still experimental

    A Kinect reads how a performer moves. The idea is that when she comes near the plant, the arm moves to meet her there. Wekinator may sit in between to learn her poses.

  4. The loop

    Frippertronics means looping over yourself, the way Robert Fripp and Brian Eno did with two tape machines. Here the loop can take a live performer, the plant's own notes, or the performer's body read by the Kinect and played like a theremin.

Escape the Grid

A plant, a robot arm, a performer and a Frippertronics-style tape delay, played as one performance. Clocks exist, but the looping is timey-wimey. Echo Garden is being built at the ADE Lab Hackathon in Amsterdam, 21 to 23 October 2026, for Colin Benders' challenge, Escape the Grid.

Looking for genuine musicians, signal movers, digital makers and visual orchestrators of light. Technology and music aficionados are welcome too.
