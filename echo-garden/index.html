Echo Garden

A gridless instrument for live sets, where the music moves when bodies and plants move.

Picture a potted plant on a wooden table. A small dark box sits beside it, with one cable clipped to a leaf and one pushed into the soil. A desktop robot arm reaches over and rests its tool on a leaf. The box lights up, notes rise from the plant, and slow rings spread from the pot. The arm lifts, the notes stop, and the rings fade.

A robot arm rests its tip on a leaf, and the plant joins the band. A little box called a Pocket Scion listens to the plant's electrical signal and turns it into MIDI notes, which play instruments in a live Ableton set. When the arm lifts, the plant goes quiet, but what it already played keeps going round in the loop.

How it works

  1. The plant

    Two clips wire the plant to the Scion, one on a leaf and one in the soil. The Scion only plays on its own pulse, a little under eight times a second. A threshold decides how many of those notes get through, and the sting and a few other instruments in the set follow the same threshold.

  2. The arm

    Touching a leaf closes the plant's circuit and lets its notes in. Lifting off stops them. Each spot the arm can reach plays a different set of notes, and in every new room the arm has to learn where the leaves are before the show.

  3. The body, still experimental

    A Kinect watches the performer. When she walks up to the plant, the arm should come to meet her at the nearest leaf. Wekinator learns her poses and how restless the plant is, and uses both to conduct the loop. The projector lights her body as she moves.

  4. The loop

    Frippertronics is looping over yourself through a long tape delay, the way Robert Fripp and Brian Eno did it with two tape machines. Here the plant's notes land on three rings that loop every 3.1, 5.3 and 8.6 seconds. Those lengths don't divide into each other, so the rings drift apart and never line up again. A note gets a little quieter each lap until it's gone, and new notes pile on top. The performer can sing into the loop too, or play it with her hands like a theremin.

How a touch becomes sound

The hackathon build is the conductor. Wekinator listens to the plant and the performer and decides how full each ring plays and how long its notes hang on. She steers it with gestures and with her voice, through the mic she sings into. Anything she says waits for the end of the phrase, unless she says "now". From the desk, I can push the energy up or down with a Pebble watch.
