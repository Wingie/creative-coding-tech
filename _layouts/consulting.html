Sovereign AI Infrastructure

Your models, your hardware, your jurisdiction.

I run training and 30B-class inference on my own machines: a k3s control plane, GPU workers joined over a private network, models served locally, retrieval in my own database. I've also fixed the auth and SSRF holes in the gateway I use to do it. So I can tell you which workloads genuinely move off hosted APIs, what each costs to move, and which ones aren't worth it — which is a different answer from most people selling sovereignty.

  • On-premise and self-hosted LLM serving — sizing, quantisation, and what fits on hardware you already own
  • Private GPU orchestration across mixed hardware and locations
  • Fine-tuning small models to do a specific job a large hosted one is overqualified for
  • EU AI Act, GDPR and data-residency constraints as architecture, not paperwork
  • Honest cost modelling against the hosted alternative, including when hosted wins

AI Evaluation & Governance

Knowing whether it works, and being able to show your working.

I build evaluation harnesses designed so a model can't fake its way through — tasks gated behind the filesystem, tokens that only surface if the model actually read the data. The harness I run found two bugs that were flattering its own results. That's the job: making a benchmark that can embarrass you.

  • Task-based evaluation harnesses for agents and tool-use, not just output scoring
  • Contamination and provenance checks — did this model see your held-out set?
  • Pre-registration and sealing, so the bar can't move between the run and the report
  • Dual-use assessment: what to publish, what to withhold, and writing the reasoning down
  • Reading an existing eval suite and telling you where it's lying to you

Currently studying toward the IAPP AI Governance Professional certification.
