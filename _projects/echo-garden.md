A robot arm rests its tip on a leaf, and the plant joins the band. A Pocket Scion listens to the plant's electrical signal and turns it into MIDI, which plays instruments in my live Ableton set. When the arm lifts, the plant goes quiet.

A threshold decides how many of the plant's notes get through, and a few of my other instruments follow the same threshold.

Everything can go round a Frippertronics-style tape loop: the plant's notes, a singer's voice, or a performer playing it by hand like a theremin.

The performer plays a theremin in the air, in four ways, always in the key of the set. Hand signs switch between the drums, the bass and the lead, and on the bass or the drums the hands shape the dub instead of playing notes. The lead rings on in two long reverb loops. A small mic replaces the foot pedals: saying "make a sample of dusty dub chords" makes a new loop on the spot, in tempo and key, and two thumbs up mixes it in.

Still being tried: bringing the arm to the performer at the nearest leaf, lighting the body with the projector, and spoken words that change the visuals.

At the ADE Lab Hackathon in Amsterdam, 21 to 23 October 2026, I'm building the conductor for Colin Benders' Escape the Grid challenge. The plant's notes land on three rings that loop every 3.1, 5.3 and 8.6 seconds, so they drift apart and never line up again. Each note gets a little quieter every lap until it's gone. Wekinator learns how each performer moves, so the hand signs read the same for whoever is playing. Come and hear it: the jury presentations are on Thursday 22 October at 16:00.
